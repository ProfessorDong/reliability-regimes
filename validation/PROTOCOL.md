# Cross-family validation protocol

Recorded on November 19, 2026, before computing any of the additional targets’ reliability endpoints. This is an internal analysis specification for a revision. The initial five-target data and results were already known. Target eligibility comes from an earlier project that used the additional target files to study a different question, few-shot transfer.

## Cohort and measurements

Include all 11 targets from `drug_discovery/theranostics_current/curate_chembl_v8.py` outside the original five. They are 5-HT2A, MOR, KOR, CB2, A2A, FAAH, COX-2, AChE, EGFR, VEGFR2, and hERG. These are distinct target tasks, including related receptors and kinases. Each model uses only its own target's data. A molecule may occur in more than one target. The extraction scanned up to 16,000 ChEMBL records per target for IC50, Ki, Kd, or EC50 in nM, accepted exact, approximate, and missing relations, excluded values outside the admissible log concentration range, and took median pActivity by canonical SMILES. The archives retain only SMILES and a column historically called pIC50. Parent aggregation below is the median of the available structure-level medians, which cannot recover a record-weighted median. The source composition, relation, assay, and observation dates cannot be reconstructed from these two-column files. Report input SHA-256 values and all exclusions.

Apply the original normalization, largest-fragment selection, uncharging, and sanitization to each structure. Collapse identical standard InChIKeys within target. Sort the distinct parent keys by SHA-256 of the UTF-8 string `CommsChem-v1|` followed by the parent InChIKey (ties sorted by key). Retain the first 1,200 per target, or all if fewer. This bounded, balanced cohort prevents large tasks dominating computation and gives the models comparable training-set sizes. No outcome enters selection. Use 1,024-bit radius-2 Morgan fingerprints without chirality, and carry forward stereochemistry in the keys.

## Model and starting leads

For each target sort selected rows by parent InChIKey and shuffle five KFold splits with random state 0. Fit a 300-tree random forest to each training fold (seed 0, squared error, bootstrap, all features, minimum leaf size two). Keep one out-of-fold prediction and one population standard deviation of tree predictions per held-out parent. Define D as 1 minus maximum fingerprint Tanimoto similarity to the full training fold. Define N as the same distance to ten distinct training-fold actives, with active defined as pActivity at least 7. If a fold has fewer than ten, use all available actives and disclose the number. A fold with zero eligible leads contributes only to analyses that do not require lead novelty. Generate 100 lead-set draws, with NumPy default_rng seeded by `SeedSequence([20261119, fold, draw])`. Targets share the deterministic sampling scheme, with each draw operating on the target's actual training fold.

## Primary endpoint

The question is whether proximity to a training analogue predicts lower error among compounds equally unusual relative to the starting leads. Within each target, fold and lead draw, take test compounds at or above the within-fold two-thirds quantile of N. Stratify that third into five approximately equal-count bins using quantiles of N, keeping ties together. In each bin split D at its median (near <= median, far > median). Use every bin with both groups; weight near and far equally across the same distribution of bins, proportional to each retained bin's total size across folds and draws. Aggregate squared errors on each side with these weights and report sqrt(MSE_far) minus sqrt(MSE_near) in pActivity. Positive values indicate lower RMSE for near compounds. This coarsened stratification reduces residual differences in N; it does not exactly match individual compounds. Report the weighted mean N on both sides and paired distance and activity diagnostics. Retain the group weight of each test compound averaged over the repeated lead sets. The repeats integrate over lead choice and are never treated as independent observations.

Report the effect and a conditional percentile interval for each target, resampling the groups of test compounds defined by their Bemis–Murcko scaffolds 2,000 times with replacement, with the model, predictions, folds, lead draws, strata and compound weights held fixed. All acyclic compounds share the empty-scaffold group. Summarize all targets equally using the arithmetic mean of target effects and a two-sided 95% t interval over the 11 observed target effects. Target families and chemistry can overlap, so the interval describes dispersion under a working independent-target assumption. Report counts with a positive sign, target-level results, and means by broad target class to expose heterogeneity. No claim about prevalence in all protein targets follows from this convenience panel.

## Secondary endpoints

For each target compute pooled out-of-fold Spearman correlations of absolute error with forest disagreement, with D, and with N. For N-dependent results report medians over lead draws, including partial Spearman for disagreement conditional on D and N and for N conditional on D. Report RMSE across all predictions and after retaining the lowest-disagreement fifth. Reproduce the original descriptive high-novelty restriction as a sensitivity analysis: within the highest-novelty third pooled across folds, split D at its target-wide median and report the fraction of the 100 lead draws for which the nearer group has lower RMSE. Retain all per-compound fold assignments, measurements, predictions, full-training distances, scaffold labels and primary analysis weights. Numerical consistency checks must verify distinct keys within each modeled target, complete disjoint fold prediction coverage, nested-reference distance inequality, the declared sample and fit counts, near/far weighting, and traceability of each reported summary.
